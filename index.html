The Problem

Most landlords don't know where to start — and end up overspending

From 2030, every rental property in England and Wales needs to be rated EPC C or above. Properties that don't comply cannot legally be let — meaning no rental income until the work is done and certified.

The problem isn't the deadline. It's what happens when landlords try to deal with it without proper guidance. They go straight to a contractor, spend money on things that either don't move the score or make later improvements more expensive, and still end up short of C.

Contractors are paid to install things. They're not paid to tell you that a cheaper measure would achieve the same result. Without an independent assessment first, you're relying on someone with a financial interest in the answer to tell you what you need.

  • Non-compliant properties become unlettable

    From 2030, you will not be able to legally let a property rated below EPC C. That means zero rental income until works are certified.

  • Fines of up to £30,000 per property

    Local authorities are being given enforcement powers. For a landlord with five properties, non-compliance could mean £150,000 in fines.

  • Contractor prices will rise sharply before 2030

    Millions of landlords face the same deadline. As demand spikes close to 2030, so will costs. Landlords who act early consistently pay less for the same works.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the 2030 EPC C requirement confirmed?

The government has confirmed the direction of travel. Legislation is still being finalised but the 2030 deadline for all tenancies and 2028 for new tenancies is the working framework. Landlords who act now will be in the best position regardless of the exact final date.

What does the £250 cover?

The on-site survey, the EPC Roadmap report showing the cheapest route to C, a consultation call to go through the findings, and the updated EPC certificate lodged on the government register once works are completed.

Do you cover my area?

We currently cover Birmingham and Luton. If you're unsure whether your property falls within our coverage, call us and we'll confirm. We're expanding coverage as demand grows.

My property is rated D — how much work is actually needed?

It depends on the specific score. A high-D property might only need one or two low-cost improvements to reach C. Our survey tells you exactly where you stand and the most efficient path forward.

Can I phase the improvements over time?

Yes. The roadmap shows a priority order so you can plan works across 12–24 months if needed. The key is knowing which measures to tackle first for maximum score impact.

Why not just go straight to a contractor?

Contractors are paid to install things — not to find you the cheapest path. Without an independent assessment first, you risk spending on measures that don't move the score, or doing things in the wrong order.
